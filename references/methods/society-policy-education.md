# Society, public policy, and education research

Use for demographics, public opinion, social issues, public programs, education systems, and policy outcomes.

Define the population, geography, period, concepts, and intended comparison. Prefer official statistics, original survey documentation, peer-reviewed studies, policy evaluations, and primary public records.

For surveys, examine sample frame, size, selection, weighting, mode, field dates, question wording, nonresponse, and uncertainty. For social statistics, check definitions, coverage, revisions, and comparability. For policy evaluation, distinguish outputs from outcomes and consider counterfactuals, confounders, implementation, and distributional effects.

Represent disagreement fairly. Do not infer an entire population's views from anecdotes or unrepresentative online discussion.
