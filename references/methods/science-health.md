# Science and health research

Use for scientific questions, literature reviews, public health, and clinical evidence.

Identify the question, population or system, intervention or exposure, comparator, outcomes, and time frame where relevant. Choose an appropriate review approach. Do not call a search a systematic review unless search sources, dates, queries, eligibility criteria, screening, and synthesis are documented and reproducible.

Assess study design, sample, controls, measurement, bias, statistical and practical significance, uncertainty, replication, conflicts of interest, and applicability. Distinguish animal, laboratory, observational, and clinical evidence. Give systematic reviews and high-quality guidelines appropriate weight, while checking their date and methods.

For health topics, prioritize current clinical guidelines, regulators, systematic reviews, and well-designed studies. Distinguish population-level evidence from individual care. Do not diagnose, prescribe, or replace a qualified clinician.
