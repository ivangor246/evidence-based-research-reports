# Research core

## Scope
Define the question, purpose, audience, subject, geography, period, terms, constraints, and intended use. State assumptions.

## Search
Create as many testable subquestions as the task needs. Search varied terms, source types, and viewpoints. Include counter-evidence. Open original material and record dates.

## Claim-to-source check
For each important or decision-critical claim ask:
1. Does the source support this exact statement?
2. Do its population, geography, period, and definitions match?
3. Does it support the whole claim or only part?
4. Is it direct evidence, interpretation, marketing, or anecdote?
5. Is it independent of the other cited sources?

Split compound claims when needed. Never use a general industry statistic to prove demand in a narrow segment. A validator can check citation IDs, not semantic accuracy; review the match manually.

## Analysis
Compare like with like. Verify calculations, units, assumptions, and sensitivity. Separate observation, calculation, interpretation, hypothesis, and unknown. Do not infer causation from correlation without a suitable design.

## Synthesis
Answer directly. Present the strongest evidence, meaningful disagreements, alternatives, risks, and next steps. Remove tangents, duplication, generic background, and unsupported claims.

## Report
Keep the summary brief and readable; about 30 seconds is a guide, not a fixed limit. Put the answer first. Avoid repeating findings in the body. For many options, summarize all, analyze a shortlist, and examine only the strongest few in depth. Put supporting detail and full data in appendices when useful.
