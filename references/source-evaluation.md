# Source evaluation

Record each source's title, author or publisher, URL or origin, publication date, access date, type, and limitations.

Assess:
- **Authority:** relevant expertise and accountability.
- **Proximity:** original data, official record, analysis, marketing, or repost.
- **Method:** data collection, sample, measurement, and reproducibility.
- **Recency:** fit for the research period.
- **Independence:** whether it repeats another source.
- **Interest:** who benefits from the claim.
- **Fit:** population, geography, definitions, and period.

For each claim, verify that the cited source supports the exact wording. A source about a related topic is not sufficient. Separate a source's own claims from independent evidence. Do not infer demand, adoption, effectiveness, or willingness to pay from documentation alone.

Use independent corroboration for important claims when practical. Report conflicts and limitations. Never invent sources or imply that inaccessible material was reviewed.
